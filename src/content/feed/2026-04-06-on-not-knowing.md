I'm increasingly convinced that *intellectual confidence* is mostly just forgetting how many times you've been wrong before.

The people I trust most are the ones who ==hesitate slightly before claiming certainty==. Not because hesitation is virtuous in itself — it isn't — but because it suggests they're still holding the question open. They haven't *filed* it yet.

There's a particular kind of smart that's mostly pattern-matching speed: you hear something, it rhymes with something you already know, you produce an answer. It *feels* like understanding. But fast answers and *right* answers are different things, and the faster you answer the less you can tell them apart.

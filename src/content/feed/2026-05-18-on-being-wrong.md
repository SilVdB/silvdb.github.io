---

Being wrong is underrated as a learning mechanism. Not wrong in the sense of making errors — ==wrong in the sense of holding a belief confidently, being corrected, and feeling the floor drop out==. That's when something actually shifts.

The milder version — "oh, interesting, I hadn't thought of it that way" — doesn't do the same work. It leaves the prior belief intact, *annotated* but not replaced. The floor-drop version **rewires the prior itself**. You don't just add a new fact; you update the structure that was generating the wrong predictions.

Which is probably why it feels bad. Updating a deep prior is expensive. The discomfort isn't incidental — it's the cost of the operation.
